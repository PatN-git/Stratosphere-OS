## 1
When a job is ingested it gets a 0 to 100 score: 85 or more goes live, below 85 stays a draft. Even at 85 or more it is held as a draft if it has a disqualifier: an invalid or known-failing apply link, a talent-pool posting, a fallback to the company's portal page instead of a direct link, or an unverified category. A job in a foreign location (with no German match) is archived on arrival, unless an unreachable link takes priority and makes it a draft, and a job already live in the database is not demoted by a re-scan. Live jobs are archived later when they are missing from a company's scan twice (a same-title, same-region job that reappears under a new link is relinked instead), or when the daily link check finds a 404/410, a "position filled" style page, a posting older than 180 days, no apply link, or a failure count that reaches 2 on server errors or other non-success responses.
## 2
The job gets the 10 points unless its apply link is explicitly flagged as not working (the "link returns HTTP 200" flag set to false). The sources I read either set that flag to true or leave it unset, so the 10 points are given by default, and nothing in the ingestion code sets it to false. A link that is not a valid http or https address does not just miss the 10: the whole score becomes 0. Separately, a link that looks like a direct job URL earns 15 points and one that does not earns 5.
## 3
The department counts for one third of the title. A title match scores 15, 7.5 or 3.75 points depending on the strength of the keyword group, while a department match scores 5, 2.5 or 1.25, and the department points are added for a group only if the title did not already match that group. A "negative" keyword found in either the title or the department subtracts 15. The best-scoring category wins but needs at least 4 points, otherwise the job is "other"; some sources (for example Ashby, Personio, Workable, Recruitee, Join, Gemini) pass the department in, while others (for example Greenhouse, HiBob, Teamtailor) pass the title alone.
## 4
They are read from the source's own fields where the job feed has them, and otherwise from the job title, using keyword lists. For example, work mode comes from a workplace-type field or the location text and then the title (words like remote, hybrid, on-site, office), job type comes from an employment-type field or the title (student/intern, full-time, part-time, freelance, co-founder), and seniority comes mostly from title words such as intern, junior, senior, lead, head of or director, with a few sources also supplying a seniority or experience field. If nothing is recognised, the saved job is filled in with the defaults "experienced", "full-time" and "hybrid".
## 5
When the AI extractor (Gemini) gets a quota error (429) for a company, it marks itself out of quota for 60 seconds, stops trying further AI models for that company and returns no jobs. In the nightly run a new extractor is created for each company, so that 60-second flag does not carry over to the next company; it covers just that one company's extraction. Because the extractor returns an empty result rather than raising an error, the run's own quota stop, which reacts to a raised 429-type error, is not triggered by it. The key pool keeps its own separate 60-second timeout on the rate-limited key.
## 6
Before each call the pool picks the key with the fewest uses among keys that are not in a timeout (ties go to the least recently used), so calls spread across keys even without errors. During a call it switches when Google answers 429: that key is put in a 60-second timeout and the next key is tried, for at most 3 tries (4 if a paid reserve key exists, and no more than the number of keys). Other failures, such as a network error, are not retried on another key, and a timeout comes back as a result without switching. If the last allowed try also gets a 429, the call fails with a 429 error.
## 7
The paid key is picked when no free key is out of its timeout at the moment a key is chosen, meaning all five free keys have recently returned 429 and are within their 60-second timeout. While any free key is available it is preferred over the paid one. One call tries at most 4 keys when a paid key exists, so on a fresh pool where every key returns 429, the first call uses four free keys and fails without reaching the paid key; the next call tries the fifth free key and, once it also gets a 429, moves to the paid key. If the paid key is itself in a timeout, the pool falls back to the free key whose timeout ends soonest.
## 8
It is a plain "contains" match that ignores upper and lower case: "solar" matches a company whose name, tags, city or sector name has the letters "solar" anywhere inside, even in the middle of a word (a name such as "Solarize" would match, as an illustration). You can separate several terms with commas, and a company matches if it contains at least one of them; a phrase with spaces is treated as one term. It runs in the browser on the loaded company list, together with the selected filters, and the company description is not searched.
## 9
The banner appears after the visitor has entered a website and clicked AI Auto-fill, when the lookup finds a company whose website contains that domain (the first match) and that company's status is draft. If the matched company is not a draft, the visitor sees the "Already Registered" notice instead, and no match, or a lookup that fails or takes longer than 3 seconds, shows neither. When the site's profile is served from the 90-day cache, no fresh lookup is made and the match result stored at the time of caching is reused, so the banner can reflect an older status. A later auto-fill that finds no match removes it.
## 10
When a commit is made, the gate looks at the staged files (compared with the last commit) and requires each one to match a recorded passing test run: its exact content must be what the tests passed on, and a deleted file must have been recorded as deleted. It skips documentation and text files (.md, .mdx, .txt, .rst), anything under docs/, .memory/, .agents/, .claude/ or .github/ from the repository root, LICENSE files, .gitignore, .gitattributes, common image files (png, jpg, jpeg, gif, svg, webp, ico), and symlinks and submodules. Everything else, including test files and config files, is checked, and a file edited after the last passing run blocks the commit. This applies where the hook has been installed, and the record is written when a test run passes.
## 11
It looks at the test files in the change, from the branch's fork point with the default branch to the working tree, and counts lines that look like assertions (assert..., expect(, .should, t.Error or t.Fatal, require., raises(, toThrow) added versus removed. It flags a problem when the total across all test files is a net loss, that is, more such lines removed than added, and then it lists each test file that lost more than it gained. Moving or rewriting assertions that net to zero or more is not flagged, and neither is a loss in one file that another test file makes up. A "tests-removed" row under "Floor exceptions" in .memory/ARCHITECTURE.md, merged to the default branch, can allow it.
## 12
It compares the removed and added lines in specific config files: pytest.ini, pyproject.toml, setup.cfg, tox.ini, .coveragerc, package.json and GitHub workflow files (coverage minimums such as fail_under and --cov-fail-under, and the --max-warnings or maxWarnings limit), jest or vitest config and package.json (lines, branches, functions, statements), and codecov files (target, threshold). It flags a minimum that was lowered or removed, and a max-warnings limit or codecov threshold that was raised or removed; an unchanged value, a tighter one, or a brand-new limit with no previous value is not flagged, and files outside those lists, such as a separate ESLint config, are not scanned. A "threshold" row under "Floor exceptions" in .memory/ARCHITECTURE.md, merged to the default branch, can allow it.
## 13
No, it requires a different version, not a higher one. For a document that already existed at the pull request's fork point with the default branch, the validator reports an error if its content changed (compared without its version and timestamp lines) while its version string is still the same as at the fork point; any other version value passes this check, even a lower one. Documents that did not exist at the fork point are not compared this way, and each version must be in the x.y.z form. A separate release guard on pull requests does require the overall plugin version in build.py to be higher than the last release tag when shipped content changed.
## 14
It depends on which line. The content fingerprint ignores exactly the frontmatter lines named "version" and "timestamp", so editing only a "timestamp:" date does not count as a content change and needs no version bump. A date in the newer "generated: at:" line, or a date in the body text, is part of the fingerprint, so changing it does count as a content change. Differences in line endings (CRLF versus LF) and a leading byte-order mark are also ignored.
