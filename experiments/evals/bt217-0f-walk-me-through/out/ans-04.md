## 1
Each job found on a company's career page gets a confidence score from 0 to 100: 85 or more with no red flag goes live, anything below 85 is a draft. A red flag keeps even a high-scoring job a draft: a link that is not a valid web address or is flagged unreachable, an open-application or talent-pool title, a portal-only link, or an unverified category. The exception is a job located outside Germany (with no German region match): that one is archived straight away, unless the link problem outranks it, in which case it is a draft. Later, a live job is archived by the daily link check when its page returns 404 or 410, shows "position filled" style text, has no apply link, or was posted more than 180 days ago; a second failed check (server error or other non-success reply such as 403) also archives it, while a network outage leaves it unchanged. A live job is also archived when it is missing from the company's career page in two scans in a row, and a re-scan does not turn a stored live job back into a draft.
## 2
Those 10 points are the "link is not known to be dead" points: the job gets them unless it is explicitly flagged as not reachable. Nothing in the ingestion code sets that flag to false, and the scoring step never opens the link, so in practice a job with a valid web link gets them. A job whose apply link is not a valid http or https address does not just lose the 10 points, its whole score is 0. Separately, the link earns 15 points if it looks like a direct job page (for example containing /jobs or /careers) and only 5 otherwise, and a portal-only link caps the total at 80.
## 3
The title counts three times as much as the department. A keyword match in the title earns 15, 7.5 or 3.75 points for the three keyword tiers, while the same kind of match found only in the department earns 5, 2.5 or 1.25. The department is only looked at for a tier when the title did not already match that tier, and a blocking word in either the title or the department takes 15 points off a category. The highest-scoring category wins but needs at least 4 points, otherwise the job is "other". Not every job source passes a department: Ashby, Join, Personio, Recruitee, Workable, the page-selector and the Gemini extraction do, while Greenhouse, Deel, HeavenHR, HiBob, TalentStorm and TeamTailor classify on the title alone.
## 4
They are worked out by the connector for each job board (Ashby, Greenhouse, Personio and so on) at the moment the job is read in. Where the board supplies its own field (for example Ashby's workplace type and employment type) that text is tried first, and otherwise or if it is not recognised the job title is searched for keywords such as "remote", "hybrid", "Werkstudent", "Senior" or "Junior". If still nothing is recognised, the stored job gets the defaults: hybrid work mode, "experienced" seniority and full-time job type. Those defaults are filled in after the score is calculated, so a defaulted field does not earn its confidence points.
## 5
When the AI call fails with a quota error (429, or an error text mentioning "quota"), that Gemini extractor stops trying other models, returns no jobs, and refuses further AI work for 60 seconds. In the nightly run, however, a brand-new extractor is created for every company, so the pause is not shared: it only covers the rest of that one company's extraction, and the next company starts with no pause. The extractor also returns an empty list rather than raising an error, so it does not trigger the run-wide "quota exhausted" stop. The routine that would skip remaining companies during the pause exists but the nightly script does not call it.
## 6
It switches in two ways. Before every call it picks, among the free keys not currently in a cooldown, the least-used one (ties go to the least recently used), so calls spread across keys. After a call comes back as a 429 rate-limit error (a returned 429, or an error mentioning 429 or RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED), that key is put on a 60-second cooldown and the same request is retried on another key, up to 3 attempts (4 when a paid reserve key is configured) and no more than the number of keys. Other failures such as timeouts or other error codes do not cause a switch.
## 7
The paid key is used only when no free key is outside its cooldown at the moment a key is chosen, and only if the paid key itself is not in cooldown. A single request makes at most 4 attempts when a paid key exists, so if all five free keys were fresh and every call returned 429, that request would use four free keys and fail without reaching the paid key or the fifth free key. On the next request within 60 seconds the fifth free key would be tried; if that one also returns 429, all five free keys are cooling down and the paid key is tried next. If the paid key is also cooling, it picks the free key whose cooldown ends soonest instead.
## 8
The search runs in the browser on the already-loaded company list as you type. It lowercases what you typed and checks for that text anywhere inside the company's name, any of its tags, its city, or its sector label, so "solar" would match a company called "SolarWorks" or a tag like "solar-panels" as well as a whole word "solar". Several words separated by commas match a company if any one of them matches, and the company description is not searched. A company must also pass any sector, team-size, funding and "hiring now" filters that are switched on.
## 9
The banner appears only after the visitor has entered a website and clicked "AI Auto-fill", and the answer says a company with that website already exists in the database with the status "draft". The lookup is a "contains" match on the website's domain and takes the first match only; if that company is already published, the visitor sees the "Already Registered" warning instead. If the AI profile for that domain was found in the 90-day saved cache, the draft lookup is not run again and the banner depends on what was stored with that cached profile. A failed or rate-limited auto-fill request does not show or change the banner.
## 10
It checks the files staged for the commit (compared with the last commit), including deleted files, and treats each as needing proof that it was in a passing test run with identical content. Skipped are documentation and framework files: *.md, *.mdx, *.txt, *.rst, anything under docs/, .memory/, .agents/, .claude/ and .github/, LICENSE files, .gitignore, .gitattributes, and common image files (png, jpg, jpeg, gif, svg, webp, ico). Symlinks and submodules are also skipped. The commit is blocked if a checked file is missing from the recorded run or its recorded content differs from what is staged, and this only happens if the pre-commit hook has been installed.
## 11
It looks at test files (files in tests/, test/, __tests__/ or spec/ folders, test_* files, *_test.* files, and .test or .spec files, plus Java Tests files), comparing the whole branch with where it split from the main branch, including uncommitted and new files. It counts lines that contain an assertion (such as assert, expect(, .should, raises( or toThrow) added versus removed. It flags only when, across all test files together, more assertion lines were removed than added, and then it reports each test file that individually lost more than it gained. A listed exception for "tests-removed" merged on the default branch can allow it.
## 12
It flags a change when a coverage or warning limit is loosened or deleted: a removed line carrying such a limit and no added line that is as strict. Coverage limits are fail_under or --cov-fail-under in pytest.ini, pyproject.toml, setup.cfg, tox.ini, .coveragerc, package.json or GitHub workflow files; lines, branches, functions or statements in jest or vitest config files or package.json; and target or threshold in a codecov file. Lowering a coverage number, or raising a max-warnings or codecov threshold number, is flagged, as is removing the setting entirely. Tightening is not flagged, and a limit that is newly added with no old line removed is not flagged either, and an exception row for "threshold" on the default branch can allow it.

For each built document that already existed at the point where the branch split from the main branch, if its content fingerprint changed and its version number is still identical, the validator reports "content changed but version not bumped". ## 14
It depends on which date line. The "timestamp:" line in a document's header is deliberately left out of the content comparison (along with the "version:" line), so editing only that line is not a content change. A date inside the "generated:" block of the header (the "at:" line, used for example in the memory templates) or a date in the document's body is part of the compared content, so changing only that does count and would require a version bump.
